05 — COMMUNITY

Showing up beyond the classroom

Experiences that connected me with environmental, social, and community-focused work.

01

Bagmati River Clean-Up

MALPI OUTREACH PROGRAM

Joined 70+ volunteers in a river-area clean-up, collecting 7–8 large bags of waste in a single day. Seeing the amount of waste firsthand made environmental responsibility feel much more immediate than simply discussing it. It reminded me that caring for the environment is not only a long-term goal — it is also a responsibility that shows up in the small, practical choices we make every day.

One of the most memorable moments came when passersby began joining the clean-up on their own. What started as a planned volunteer activity became something the surrounding community participated in, showing me how visible action can encourage others to get involved.

02

Cancer Awareness Cycling Rally

VOLUNTEER CYCLIST

Participated as a volunteer cyclist in a breast cancer awareness campaign with 200+ participants. I joined because I wanted to contribute to a form of awareness that reached people before the issue became personal to them or their families.

What stayed with me was how the rally turned movement into communication. Along the route, I spoke with a passerby about the purpose of the campaign and why awareness mattered. It was a small interaction, but it showed me that community work does not always require a large gesture — sometimes it begins with simply starting a conversation.

03

Preschool Volunteering

HAPPY HOME MONTESSORI

Volunteered at Happy Home Montessori, supporting young children with early learning activities including letters, music, coloring, and everyday routines. Being an elder sister made the environment feel familiar, but working with a classroom of young children taught me to approach that responsibility with greater patience and attentiveness.

I especially enjoyed seeing the moment when a child finally understood something they had been struggling with. Alongside teaching, I gained insight into the quieter responsibilities of working with children — listening carefully, adapting to different needs, and creating an environment where they felt comfortable learning. I also experienced the administrative side of running a preschool, but teaching was where I found myself most engaged.

VISUAL ART

Mandala Ink · Elephant Mandala

This piece started with something surprisingly ordinary: lockdown boredom. I began doodling, decided to try mandala art, and quickly realized it wasn't nearly as easy as I had imagined. But once I understood the process, I fell in love with it.

Mandala art is slow by nature. I start by sketching loosely in pencil, planning the overall pattern, building each section detail by detail, and then tracing everything carefully in ink. This piece took almost five days to complete, and I genuinely enjoyed every moment of it.

This particular piece depicts an elephant, worked entirely through mandala patterning. A distant relative saw an earlier mandala piece of mine that my dad had posted online and asked me to make one for her. I did, and eventually the artwork made its way all the way to her home in America.

MUSIC · VOCALS

Finding My Voice

This started with my dad, who used to sing at home and made it look effortless enough that I wanted to try it myself. I sang, but for a long time I didn't really trust that I was any good at it.

That changed at school. Jamming casually with friends and singing without thinking too much about it, people started telling me I was actually good. That encouragement pushed me into inter-house singing competitions, where my house placed second twice.

Over time, singing became more than something I participated in myself. I took on the role of lead singer and performance coordinator, holding the microphone while also helping coordinate the overall performance. I voluntarily supported other members during rehearsals, helping them prepare and bringing the group together.

Those experiences were the first time singing felt like something I could really do, rather than simply something I enjoyed. They taught me that confidence doesn't always come before you try something — sometimes it grows because you keep putting yourself out there.

MUSIC

Guitar · Harmonica

Giving the Songs a Backbone

Singing alone always felt incomplete to me — like something was missing underneath it. Guitar became the obvious answer.

I'd wanted to learn from a young age but never got the chance, so instead I pushed my brother to learn it. Watching him become genuinely excellent at it became its own kind of inspiration.

Eventually, I picked it up myself — partly chasing my favorite songs, and partly because playing guitar alone is its own quiet kind of entertainment. I can sit, play, sing, and enjoy my own company.

The first song I ever sang and played at the same time was "Stand By Me," a song I'd wanted to learn for a long time. That moment — singing and playing simultaneously for the first time — is still a core memory for me.

Harmonica came from one very specific place: the harmonica line in "Piano Man." I loved that part of the song enough to want to learn how to play it myself, and that's where it started.

DANCE

Growing Up With Dance

Dance has been part of my life for as long as I can remember — the kind of thing you're introduced to early, especially as a girl, and then it simply stays with you. My school had dance classes from a very young age, and I danced even before that, back in preschool.

I've never quite had the confidence to compete much. The closest I've come was a group competition a long time ago, where we placed second. But confidence to compete was never really the point for me.

What I love about dance is what it lets me do without needing an audience or a placement: express myself through movement and music, whenever I get the chance.

For me, dance has never really been about being the best person in the room. It's simply something that has grown alongside me and given me another way to express things that are sometimes difficult to put into words.

NAIL ART & DESIGN

Certified Nail Technician

Nail art, fashion, and design have been part of my interests since I was young. Watching my cousins create DIY nail art at home was something that inspired me and always appealed to me.

During my gap after Grade 12, I decided to turn that casual interest into an actual skill. I took nail art classes and eventually earned my certification.

What I enjoy most is bringing my art background into it — working with small, intricate designs directly onto nails. It turned out to be its own kind of patience-testing detail work, where even the smallest detail matters.
